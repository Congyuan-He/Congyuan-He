## About me

I am an epidemiology PhD student working at the intersection of **environmental epidemiology**, **Bayesian statistics**, and **causal inference**. My research focuses on how environmental exposures shape cardiovascular health and how applied statistical methods can make comparisons across exposure metrics more interpretable.

I am particularly interested in translating complex exposure data into clear, reproducible epidemiologic evidence.

## Research at a glance

| Research area                    | Current interests                                            |
| :------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🌫️ **Environmental epidemiology** | Health effects of ambient air pollution and related environmental exposures |
| 🫀 **Cardiovascular health**      | Blood pressure and hospital-based cardiovascular outcomes    |
| 📐 **Bayesian methodology**       | Joint-exposure modeling, non-decreasing structures, and posterior comparisons |
| 🧭 **Causal inference**           | Difference-in-differences and quasi-experimental evaluation of environmental policies |

## Current work

### Bayesian comparative modeling

Developing interpretable Bayesian approaches for comparing association patterns across environmental exposure metrics, with an application to long-term PM₂.₅ exposure, short-term fluctuations, and blood pressure.

### Environmental policy evaluation

Exploring modern difference-in-differences designs to evaluate whether air-pollution control policies translate into measurable cardiovascular health benefits.
